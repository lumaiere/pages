## Who Is Chicago?

Chicago is an American rock band formed in Chicago in 1967. They began as **The Chicago Transit Authority**, then shortened the name to Chicago after their debut album.

What made them unusual from the beginning was the horn section. This was not a rock band that occasionally invited a trumpet over for dinner. Brass was built directly into the architecture of the music.

That gave Chicago a sound that could move between rock, jazz-rock, pop, funk, soul, and big melodic ballads without sounding like five unrelated bands had accidentally booked the same studio.

Their [official history](https://chicagotheband.com/a-chicago-story/) tells the story nicely, including one of my favorite details about *If You Leave Me Now*: Peter Cetera's song nearly did not make the album at all. The band needed one more song.

One more song.

Then that "one more song" became their first Billboard No. 1 single.

There is probably a life lesson in there somewhere, but I am going to resist turning it into a motivational poster.

## What Is *If You Leave Me Now*?

*If You Leave Me Now* was written and sung by bassist **Peter Cetera** and appeared on [*Chicago X*](https://chicagotheband.com/js_albums/chicago-x/), released in June 1976. The single followed that summer.

Genre-wise, this is **soft rock and pop ballad territory**, even though Chicago itself came from a much broader rock and jazz-rock tradition.

The song is about exactly what the title suggests: someone realizing that a relationship may be ending and pleading with the other person not to leave.

There is regret in it. Fear. Vulnerability. The uncomfortable realization that the person walking away may be taking a huge part of your life with them.

But musically?

It floats.

The strings are gentle. The guitar is restrained. Cetera's voice almost glides over the arrangement. Nothing is trying to punch you in the chest.

That contrast is probably part of why I find it so peaceful. The subject matter is emotional panic, but the performance sounds like someone has dimmed the lights, lowered the volume of the entire world, and asked everybody to please stop moving for four minutes.

## Did This Thing Actually Become a Hit?

Oh, just a little.

*If You Leave Me Now* became **Chicago's first No. 1 single on the Billboard Hot 100** and also topped charts around the world.

In the United Kingdom, it spent **three weeks at No. 1**, according to the [Official Charts history](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/chicago-if-you-leave-me-now/).

Then came the awards.

At the 1977 Grammy Awards, Chicago won **Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus** for the song. James William Guercio and Jimmie Haskell also won **Best Arrangement Accompanying Vocalist(s)** for it. The single was nominated for **Record of the Year**, while *Chicago X* was nominated for **Album of the Year** and its packaging won its own award.

The [1977 Grammy results](https://www.grammy.com/awards/19th-annual-grammy-awards/) are one of those pages where you look at the competition and realize just how stacked popular music was at the time.

## Have I Heard It in a Movie or Show?

Very possibly.

The song has had a long second life in film and television because apparently directors also understand that few things say "emotional trouble" quite like a gorgeous soft-rock ballad.

It appears in [*Three Kings*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120188/soundtrack/), [*Shaun of the Dead*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0365748/soundtrack/), [*The Half of It*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt9683478/soundtrack/), and [*The Flash*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0439572/soundtrack/), among other productions.

That is a wonderfully strange collection of movies.

War satire. Zombie comedy. Coming-of-age romance. Superhero multiverse chaos.

Chicago apparently travels well.

## Is Chicago Known for Anything Else?

Very much so.

Even if you somehow missed *If You Leave Me Now*, there is a good chance you have heard **25 or 6 to 4**, **Saturday in the Park**, **Beginnings**, **Hard to Say I'm Sorry**, **Colour My World**, or **You're the Inspiration**.

Chicago was inducted into the [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2016](https://rockhall.com/inductees/chicago/), and their catalog is unusual because it spans multiple versions of the band without losing that identifiable Chicago DNA.

The early material can be adventurous, horn-heavy, jazzy, and muscular.

The later hits can be polished, romantic, and almost impossibly smooth.

Some fans strongly prefer one era over another.

I solve this problem with a sophisticated technical process called **listening to whichever song I feel like hearing**.

## Who Sounds Similar?

If the horn-driven side of Chicago is what grabs you, **Blood, Sweat & Tears** is an obvious place to go next.

If you like the polished melodic rock side, try **The Doobie Brothers** or **Toto**.

If what you love is the combination of elite musicianship, horns, pop instincts, and a catalog big enough to lose an afternoon inside, **Earth, Wind & Fire** is another natural stop.

And that last comparison is especially appropriate because Chicago and Earth, Wind & Fire have repeatedly toured together. Their [Heart & Soul tour](https://chicagotheband.com/news/chicago-and-earth-wind-fire-launch-heart-soul-2024-north-america-tour/) even culminated with musicians from both bands sharing the stage for the finale.

That is a lot of brass in one place.

I assume nearby trumpets started vibrating sympathetically.

Chicago also spent part of 2026 on its first co-headlining tour with Styx, during what the band described as its **59th touring season**, which you can see in the [official 2026 tour announcement](https://chicagotheband.com/js_events/chicago-styx-at-ithink-financial-amphitheatre/).

Fifty-nine touring seasons.

At that point your road cases should qualify for retirement benefits.

## Why This Song Moves Me

There are songs that energize me.

There are songs that drop me into a specific place and time.

There are songs that seem to reach directly into some emotional compartment I forgot I had.

*If You Leave Me Now* does something simpler.

It settles me.

I hear those opening moments and something inside just unclenches.

The odd thing is that I am not really listening to it as a breakup song. I know what the song is saying. I understand the emotional stakes. But the sound itself overrides the narrative for me.

It becomes calm.

Maybe that is the secret of this whole series.

Music does not have to move us in the way it was intended to move us.

A sad song can make you feel safe.

A loud song can make you peaceful.

A song about somebody leaving can somehow make everything feel, for four minutes, exactly where it belongs.

That is what *If You Leave Me Now* does for me.
