## Are We Actually in a Bear Market?

**Not by the usual S&P 500 definition, as of the November 19, 2026 close.** According to [Thursday's market report from the Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-2f178054aea79d282e5ccf8d62f26a28), the S&P 500 closed at 7,175.83, about **8.2% below its October record high**. Painful? Absolutely. A 20% bear-market decline? Not yet.

For anyone keeping score, the [S&P 500 index and its index-linked products](https://www.google.com/finance/quote/.INX:INDEXSP) offer a more official view than your uncle's group chat, where three consecutive red days constitute the end of civilization.

Here's the vocabulary Wall Street uses to make financial distress sound like a weather forecast:

- **Pullback:** A decline of less than 10% from a recent high. Unpleasant, but not yet a correction.
- **Correction:** A decline of roughly 10% to less than 20%. Your portfolio is being "corrected," apparently for the crime of existing.
- **Bear market:** A decline of at least 20% from a recent peak. **There is no required number of weeks or months**. It is about the size of the fall, not how long you have been staring at it.
- **Bull market:** Generally, a rise of at least 20% from a market low. Again, no magic number of months. It is usually obvious only after everyone has spent six weeks insisting the rally cannot possibly be real.

## A Century of Financial Emotional Damage

The fascinating numbers come from [Hartford Funds' historical look at bull and bear markets](https://www.hartfordfunds.com/practice-management/client-conversations/managing-volatility/bear-markets.html), which tracks the S&P 500 back to 1928. Over nearly a century, its figures show:

- **27 bear markets**, averaging **289 days**, or about **9.6 months**.
- **28 bull markets**, averaging **988 days**, or about **2.7 years**.
- An average **35% decline** in bear markets, versus an average **112% gain** in bull markets.
- Stocks rising roughly **78% of the time**, historically.

There is a catch: a 20% gain does not erase a previous 20% loss. If $100 falls to $80 and then rises 20%, you have $96. The missing four dollars have apparently gone to pay for Wall Street's vocabulary department.

And no, **a bear market does not automatically mean a recession**. The stock market and the broader economy are related, but they do not share a calendar, a therapist, or an obligation to behave sensibly.

Now that we have established the facts, let us do the financially responsible thing and laugh at our predicament.

## Ten Jokes for Anyone Who Has Opened a Brokerage App Since August

**1. The New Security Feature**

My brokerage app just added two-factor authentication. First it asks for my password. Then it asks, "Are you absolutely sure you want to see this?"

**2. Buy the Dip, They Said**

I bought the dip. Then I bought the dip inside the dip. Yesterday the dip opened a basement. At this point, I am not an investor. I am an archaeologist with a debit card.

**3. Tax Season Is Going to Be Awkward**

My accountant asked, "Any capital gains this year?"

I said, "No, but I have experienced tremendous personal growth."

She said, "The IRS does not accept character development."

**4. The Robo-Advisor Has Concerns**

My robo-advisor told me to stay calm, stick to my strategy, and think long-term. Then I caught it updating its résumé on LinkedIn.

**5. The Watchlist**

I renamed my stock watchlist "Unsolved Mysteries." Every company is listed under "Last seen near an all-time high."

**6. Expert Technical Analysis**

An analyst said the market has "strong support" just below current prices. I asked whether that support was structural or emotional, because at this point I need both.

**7. My Retirement Calculator**

Last year, my retirement calculator said I could stop working at 65. Today it asked, "Which lifetime?"

**8. The Joy of Diversification**

I bought a diversified fund so I wouldn't have to pick individual losers. Now they arrive prepackaged in a convenient family size.

**9. The Stop-Loss Order**

My broker asked if I wanted to set a stop-loss order. I said, "Can we start with a stop-crying order? The losses seem pretty committed."

**10. Searching for the Bottom**

A market commentator said, "Investors are still looking for a floor."

Same here. I need somewhere to lie down.

## The Good News, Which Has Somehow Survived the Meeting

History has not made market declines painless, but it has made them ordinary. Bear markets are usually shorter than bull markets, and a bad month is not a forecast of the next decade. None of this tells us what stocks will do next week. If anyone promises otherwise, ask to borrow their time machine and inspect the warranty.
