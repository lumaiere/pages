## How to Actually Unpin a TikTok Video

If you've arrived here because your own pinned videos seem to have developed permanent residency, here's the solution that worked for me.

**Use the mobile TikTok app.**

1. Open TikTok on your phone.

2. Tap **Profile**.

3. Open the video you want to unpin.

4. Tap the **three-dot menu** that opens the **Send to** panel. Depending on your app version, you may reach this panel through the Share control.

5. Look at the horizontal row of greyish action icons near the bottom of the panel.

6. Swipe horizontally through the icons if necessary.

7. Find **Unpin**.

8. Tap it.

9. Return to your profile and confirm that the video no longer has the Pinned label.

That's it.

**Profile → Pinned video → Send to / Share menu → Unpin.**

Your interface may look slightly different depending on your device and app version, but the key is to inspect those lower action icons rather than just the people and applications you can share the video with.

And here's another useful detail:

**Unpinning a video does not delete it.**

It simply removes the special placement at the top of your profile. The video remains published with its existing visibility settings.

You can now give that valuable profile space to something newer, better, or at least something you haven't been staring at for the last several months.

## Why Was This So Ridiculously Difficult?

The problem isn't necessarily that the functionality doesn't exist.

It's that the functionality can be difficult to discover.

This is a classic user experience problem.

In interface design, **discoverability** refers to how easily a user can figure out what actions are available and how to perform them.

Consider what happened here.

On the desktop, an action named "Unpin from top" appeared but did not produce the expected result in my experience.

On mobile, the Pinned label looked relevant but wasn't interactive.

The video contained several menus, and the action I needed was buried among options associated with sending or sharing content.

The result?

I spent far more time searching for a simple function than actually using it.

That's not a great design outcome.

And before anyone suggests I should have read the instructions, let me point out that a reasonably experienced computer user should not need a certification program to rearrange three videos.

**Good interface design helps users find what they need without forcing them to guess which unrelated menu contains the answer.**

Of course, TikTok isn't alone here.

Modern applications are filled with overloaded menus, hidden gestures, context-sensitive controls, and icons whose meanings are apparently transmitted telepathically to selected users.

Sometimes the functionality is perfectly fine.

It's the journey to discover it that resembles a psychological endurance test.

## What If the Unpin Option Still Doesn't Appear?

If you don't see the option in your mobile app, try a few basic troubleshooting steps.

First, make sure you're opening a video from your own profile, not someone else's post or a repost.

Then check both the Share panel and any available three-dot options menu.

Don't forget to swipe horizontally through the lower action icons. Some controls may not be visible immediately.

If the option is genuinely absent, check whether an app update is available and restart TikTok.

You can also consult the official [TikTok Help Center](https://support.tiktok.com/en) or report the issue through **Profile → Menu → Settings and privacy → Report a problem**.

And if the desktop Unpin command appears to do nothing, as it did for me, try the mobile application instead.

The mobile route was the one that finally solved my problem.

## The Bigger Lesson: Simple Things Should Be Simple

I understand that social media applications are complicated.

They have millions of users, countless features, sophisticated algorithms, privacy controls, creator tools, advertising systems, and approximately seventeen thousand different reasons to send somebody a notification.

But there is something wonderfully absurd about a platform capable of recommending videos based on my behavior with astonishing precision while simultaneously making it difficult to remove a pin from my profile.

The technology can figure out what I'm likely to watch next.

But apparently, it cannot figure out that someone searching for "Unpin" probably wants to unpin something.

I'm not asking for artificial general intelligence here.

I'm asking for a menu.

Preferably one that works.
