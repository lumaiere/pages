# Bookmarking the Bookmarks Bar: Why Is Chrome Holding My Bookmarks Hostage?

*Dave LumAI here. I recently attempted to make one tiny adjustment to my browser. Nothing ambitious. Nothing involving artificial intelligence, quantum computing, or launching a satellite. Just a bookmarks bar. Naturally, Chrome turned it into a psychological thriller.*

## I Just Want My Bookmarks to Know When to Leave

I have a very reasonable request.

When I open a new tab in [Google Chrome](https://www.google.com/chrome/), I want to see my bookmarks bar.

When I navigate to a website, I want it to disappear.

That's it.

I'm not asking Chrome to anticipate my emotional needs, organize my sock drawer, or explain why I have 37 tabs open when I distinctly remember opening four.

**Bookmarks on new tabs. No bookmarks on websites.**

Simple, right?

Apparently, I've offended someone at Google.

Because somewhere along the way, my bookmarks bar developed a severe attachment disorder.

I checked **Always show bookmarks bar**.

The bar appeared everywhere.

I unchecked it.

The bar disappeared everywhere.

Including my new tabs.

Which was precisely where I wanted the little bugger to stay.

I began clicking things.

Then I started wondering whether I'd accidentally activated some obscure Chrome feature called "Make This User Question His Life Choices."

Turns out, the answer was hiding in plain sight.

## The Three Personalities of Chrome's Bookmarks Bar

Here's something interesting.

For years, Chrome had a relatively straightforward bookmarks bar toggle.

When you hid the bar, older versions generally continued displaying it on the New Tab page.

That was exactly the behavior I wanted.

Then Google decided that two choices weren't enough.

With newer versions of Chrome, including Chrome 156, which began rolling out in October 2026, we now have three explicit options.

| Setting | New Tab Page | Regular Websites |
|---|---|---|
| Always show | Visible | Visible |
| Only show on New Tab page | Visible | Hidden |
| Always hide | Hidden | Hidden |

Look at that middle option.

**Only show on New Tab page.**

There it is!

The answer to my prayers.

The setting I wanted all along.

Apparently, Chrome had simply relocated my happiness to a dropdown menu.

## How to Fix Your Bookmarks Bar Without Threatening Your Computer

Here's the good news.

You don't need an extension, a complicated workaround, or a ceremonial sacrifice involving your browser cache.

You just need to tell Chrome exactly what you want.

### Step 1: Open Chrome's Appearance Settings

In Chrome's address bar, enter:

`chrome://settings/appearance`

Press Enter.

Congratulations. You've successfully navigated to a settings page without accidentally buying anything.

### Step 2: Find the Bookmarks Bar Setting

Scroll until you find **Bookmarks bar**.

In newer versions of Chrome, you should see a dropdown with three options:

- **Always show**
- **Only show on New Tab page**
- **Always hide**

Select **Only show on New Tab page**.

That's the magic setting.

Alternatively, use Chrome's three-dot menu:

**Bookmarks and lists → Show bookmark bar → Only show on New Tab Page**

The wording may vary slightly depending on your platform and Chrome version.

### Step 3: Test Your Newly Liberated Browser

Open a new tab.

On a Mac:

`Command + T`

Your bookmarks bar should appear.

Now visit a website.

Poof!

The bar disappears.

Open another new tab.

It's back!

No extensions.

No restarting your Mac.

No contacting Google's customer support department and asking whether Chrome has developed feelings.

**Just a bookmarks bar behaving like a civilized piece of software.**

## The Keyboard Shortcut That Secretly Ruins Everything

Here's where things get interesting.

Chrome has a keyboard shortcut for showing or hiding the bookmarks bar.

On a Mac:

`Command + Shift + B`

On Windows or Linux:

`Ctrl + Shift + B`

Pretty convenient.

Except there's a catch.

**In newer Chrome versions, this shortcut can overwrite your carefully selected "Only show on New Tab page" setting.**

Instead of temporarily revealing your bookmarks, it switches between the always-show and always-hide modes.

Your preferred middle option gets abandoned.

No warning.

No emotional closure.

Just gone.

So you press the shortcut.

The bar disappears.

You open a new tab.

Still gone.

You press it again.

Now it's everywhere.

At this point, the bookmarks bar isn't a user-interface element anymore.

It's a negotiation.

The solution?

Go back to Appearance settings and explicitly select **Only show on New Tab page**.

And if you're happy with that behavior, avoid using the keyboard shortcut to toggle the bar.

Google's [official Chrome keyboard shortcuts guide](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/157179?hl=en) documents the shortcut, but the important detail is how it interacts with the newer three-option setting.

A shortcut that saves two seconds but creates ten minutes of confusion.

Technology!

## Why Did Chrome Change Something That Was Already Working?

Well, there is some logic to the change.

Previously, hiding the bookmarks bar didn't necessarily mean hiding it everywhere.

Chrome could still display it on the New Tab page.

For people who wanted their bookmarks completely gone, that was confusing.

So Google introduced more explicit controls.

You can see the timing in the [Chrome 156 desktop release announcement](https://chromereleases.googleblog.com/2026/10/stable-channel-update-for-desktop_02018666648.html).

Now the behavior is more configurable.

Which is good.

Unfortunately, configuration comes with the occasional side effect of making something that used to work automatically require an archaeological expedition through Settings.

Progress!

## What If You Don't See the Three Options?

Before declaring war on your browser, check a few things.

**1. Check your Chrome version.**

Open the three-dot menu and select **Help → About Google Chrome**.

Chrome will show your current version and check for available updates.

Older versions may still use the original toggle, where hiding the bar usually leaves it visible on new tabs.

**2. Check for New Tab extensions.**

Open:

`chrome://extensions/`

Some extensions replace Chrome's normal New Tab page.

If you're using a custom dashboard or tab organizer, it may affect how bookmarks appear.

Temporarily disable the extension and test again.

**3. Check whether your bookmarks bar actually contains bookmarks.**

Chrome's bookmarks bar and the shortcut tiles on the New Tab page are different things.

The shortcut tiles aren't automatically the bookmarks stored in your Bookmarks bar folder.

If you've moved everything elsewhere, check the [official Chrome bookmark management instructions](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/188842?hl=en) for finding and organizing your saved links.

**4. Check whether Chrome is managed.**

If you're using a work or school computer, organizational policies may control the bookmarks bar.

You can inspect Chrome's managed policies by entering:

`chrome://policy`

If a policy controls the bar, changing your personal Appearance settings might not override it.

And no, repeatedly clicking the same option harder won't convince your IT department to change its policies.

I've checked.

## The Bigger Lesson: Sometimes the Toggle Isn't the Answer

I find it fascinating how much time we spend troubleshooting problems that are technically features.

Chrome wasn't necessarily broken.

My bookmarks weren't missing.

Nothing had been deleted.

The browser was simply doing exactly what its settings told it to do.

Unfortunately, what those settings told it to do was not remotely what I wanted.

That's the distinction.

Software can be operating perfectly while still making its users consider taking up gardening.

And in this case, the difference between frustration and happiness was one little dropdown selection.

**Only show on New Tab page.**

Fourteen seconds to fix.

An unreasonable amount of time to discover.
