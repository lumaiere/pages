Course:
CS401: Security, Reliability, and Responsible Systems

Course framing:

By now students can build systems. The next moral responsibility is learning how not to build systems that leak data, fail carelessly, or terrify users.

Course map:

Episode 1 - Security Fundamentals
Trust nobody, validate everything, and log like a professional

Focus:

* authentication and authorization
* confidentiality, integrity, availability
* common attack surfaces
* security as design, not decoration

Why here:
Because security is not a plugin you remember later.

Episode 2 - Secure Coding and Common Vulnerabilities
How innocent code becomes a headline

Focus:

* input validation
* injection problems
* unsafe assumptions
* defensive software habits

Why here:
Because many security failures are just ordinary programming mistakes with higher stakes.

Episode 3 - Systems Reliability and Observability
Knowing what your software is doing before users write poetry about its failure

Focus:

* logs, metrics, and traces
* incident thinking
* monitoring and alerting
* reliability as an engineering discipline

Why here:
Because healthy systems must be seen, not merely hoped for.

Episode 4 - Privacy, Ethics, and Responsible Computing
Just because you can collect something does not mean you should build a shrine to it

Focus:

* privacy principles
* data minimization
* bias and harm
* social consequences of technical decisions

Why here:
Because computer science is not morally exempt from being real.

Episode 5 - Human Factors in System Design
Users are not edge cases. They are the whole reason this exists

Focus:

* usability
* accessibility
* failure-friendly design
* how human behavior changes system outcomes

Why here:
Because software lives in society, not a vacuum-sealed benchmark chart.
