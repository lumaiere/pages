Now onto the next course in the series:

CS102: How programs become real systems

You have already covered the beginner slope pretty well:

* what code is
* how decisions work
* repetition and reuse
* algorithmic thinking
* data structures
* debugging and problem solving

That means the next step is moving from "I can write code" to "I understand how software is organized, runs, and survives contact with reality."

A very natural next sequence would be:

Episode 7 - Recursion and Problem Decomposition - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-7-recursion-and-problem-decomposition-or-how-to-solve-a-problem-by-making-it-smaller-66f8b6fc19f2
How to solve a problem by making it smaller until it stops fighting back

Focus:

* recursion as self-similar problem solving
* base case vs infinite sadness
* when recursion is elegant and when it is just showing off
* recursion compared to loops

Why here:
It builds directly on functions, algorithms, and step-by-step thinking.

Episode 8 - Complexity and Efficiency - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-8-complexity-and-efficiency-or-why-two-correct-programs-can-have-very-different-regret-f6825b54207a
Why two correct programs can have wildly different levels of regret

Focus:

* time and space complexity in plain English
* what Big O is really trying to tell you
* why brute force eventually becomes a public embarrassment
* practical performance intuition

Why here:
Once students know algorithms and data structures, they need to know why one choice hurts more than another.

Episode 9 - Memory and the Machine - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-9-memory-and-the-machine-or-what-the-computer-is-actually-doing-while-you-are-feeling-b7bbabe9548f
What the computer is actually doing while you are feeling confident

Focus:

* stack vs heap
* references, values, and mutation
* why memory bugs happen
* how variables live, move, and occasionally betray you

Why here:
This is where computer science stops being only syntax and starts being machinery.

Episode 10 - Files, Input, and Output - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-10-files-input-and-output-or-how-programs-stop-living-entirely-inside-their-own-heads-941dc13d407d
Programs do not live on vibes alone

Focus:

* reading and writing files
* user input
* serialization basics
* the difference between memory and storage

Why here:
This makes programs interact with the real world.

Episode 11 - Modular Design and Abstraction - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-11-modular-design-and-abstraction-or-how-to-make-your-code-less-like-a-junk-drawer-9b6273bc3721
How to make code less like a junk drawer

Focus:

* modules and interfaces
* abstraction as controlled ignorance
* separating concerns
* writing code future you will not want to fight

Why here:
This is the bridge from toy code to maintainable software.

Episode 12 - Testing and Reliability - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-12-testing-and-reliability-or-why-it-worked-once-is-not-a-career-strategy-a50f80c8d6dc
Trust is good. Tests are better.

Focus:

* what testing is actually for
* unit tests in beginner terms
* edge cases
* why "it worked once" is not a strategy

Why here:
This pairs perfectly with debugging and problem solving.

Episode 13 - State, Bugs, and Program Behavior - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-13-state-bugs-and-program-behavior-or-why-the-same-code-acts-different-after-lunch-91aeea89011d
Why the same code behaves differently after lunch

Focus:

* state changes over time
* side effects
* hidden dependencies
* tracing behavior across steps

Why here:
Students start to understand why programs become hard to reason about.

Episode 14 - Intro to Object-Oriented and Alternative Design Styles - https://medium.com/@DaveLumAI/episode-14-intro-to-object-oriented-and-alternative-design-styles-or-why-not-every-problem-needs-60bd09afa968
Not because everything must be a class, but because people keep doing that

Focus:

* objects, classes, and encapsulation
* when OOP helps
* when it becomes decorative bureaucracy
* brief contrast with procedural thinking

Why here:
It is a useful paradigm, but better after fundamentals are solid.
